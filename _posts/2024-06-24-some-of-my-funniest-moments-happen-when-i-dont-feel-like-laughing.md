*Jun 24, 2024*

HUMOR is the word I picked from my Magic Inspiration Box today.

What an odd word to pick today. As I am struggling to settle in to retirement, I don't feel very much like laughing. And yet, as I think back, some of the funniest moments occurred at just such times — when I didn't feel very much like laughing.

The moment that comes to mind is the day of my father's funeral, Monday, March 9, 1998. Bad enough that it was one of the very saddest days of my life; but also, an early spring blizzard had dumped something like 14 inches of snow onto the Chicagoland area over that weekend. Fortunately, on that day, the sun sparkled on the dunes of snow.

Following a beautiful service conducted in a packed chapel (an amazing tribute that so many people battled the snow to honor Dad), a stoic, limousine driver — straight out of Central Casting — ushered my family into his long, shiny, black limousine with two back bench seats facing each other. My mom, my brother and sister-in-law, my sister and brother-in-law, and I were seated there, knee to knee.

We were confronting a 2-hour trip to the cemetery from the funeral home because of the snow. I don't know if it was the bright sunshine that lightened our mood; if we needed to blow off some of the extreme sadness; or if it was just our typical family irreverence. Whatever it was, we started making guesses about where we thought Dad "went" on the previous Friday after he died. Mom assured us that she had opened the window in their bedroom where he died, to let his spirit out. So, we knew he had flown away somewhere.

I can't remember who said it (probably me, because I am the wittiest), but one of us said, "I think Dad went straight up to Heaven and God was showing him around the Weather Control Room. Dad said, 'Hmm, I wonder what this lever does,' and he pulled it just as God shouted, 'Nooooooo, not that one!' But too late. The lever Dad pulled was marked 'BLIZZARD'!"

Through our tears we roared! And in the rearview mirror, I could see only the limo driver's eyes looking back at us, wondering if we were crazy for laughing on such a solemn day.
